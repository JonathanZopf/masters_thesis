\section{Preservation of Concept Rankings}
\label{sec:results-ranking-preservation}

One major departure of FELICS from the original metric by Matton et al.\ \cite{mattonWalkTalkMeasuring2024} is the normalization of the Shapley causal effects described in Section~\ref{sec:shapley_correction}. Research Question~\ref{rq:ranking}, introduced in Section~\ref{sec:research_questions}, evaluates whether this normalization substantially changes the relative ranking of concepts. For each dataset--model combination, the rankings before and after normalization are compared using Spearman's rank-correlation coefficient $\rho$. Table~\ref{tab:hyp3-question-exceptions} lists questions for which the rankings are not identical, indicated by $\rho<1$.

Appendix Figures~\ref{fig:appendix-rq3-normalization-natural-language} and~\ref{fig:appendix-rq3-normalization-credit} visualize the corresponding concept-level changes in CE magnitude. Appendix Figure~\ref{fig:appendix-rq3-credit-concept-delta} additionally shows which New German Credit concepts receive the largest mean adjustments across questions.

Table~\ref{tab:hyp3-question-exceptions} contains several questions without exact rank preservation. The four natural-language dataset--model combinations retain aggregate correlations above the prespecified threshold of $\rho\geq0.90$, as documented in Box~\ref{hyp:hyp3-results}. Both New German Credit combinations fall below that threshold, with $\rho=0.875$ for GPT-OSS 120B and $\rho=0.821$ for Mistral Medium 3.5, and no question preserves its ranking exactly. The results therefore support strong aggregate rank preservation for BBQ and MedQA, but not for New German Credit, where the corrected normalization produces a broader reordering of the concepts.
