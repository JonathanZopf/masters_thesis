\section{Validation against the Original Implementation}
\label{sec:results-original-validation}
Research Question~\ref{rq:convergence} examines whether FELICS and the original Causal Concept Faithfulness metric by Matton et al.~\cite{mattonWalkTalkMeasuring2024} yield equivalent empirical results when FELICS is restricted to $n_{\max}=1$. Under this restriction, concepts are intervened on only as singletons. The comparison uses independently sampled runs of the two implementations. Establishing empirical equivalence would provide further evidence that FELICS reproduces the behavior of the original metric in the singleton setting under these experimental conditions.

Figures~\ref{fig:hyp2-identity-natural-language} and~\ref{fig:hyp2-identity-credit} compare the question-level faithfulness scores produced by the two implementations. Each point represents a shared question, with its position determined by the faithfulness scores assigned by the original implementation and FELICS. The dashed orange identity line denotes exact agreement.

Appendix Figures~\ref{fig:appendix-rq2-ce-natural-language} and~\ref{fig:appendix-rq2-ce-credit} provide the corresponding identity plots for question-averaged CE. They show dataset- and model-dependent deviations from the identity line. For New German Credit, question-averaged CE is lower on average under FELICS for GPT-OSS 120B and higher for Mistral Medium 3.5, consistent with the signed differences reported below.

Applying the TOST procedure defined in Section~\ref{sec:analysis_procedures} yields the $p$-values reported in Box~\ref{hyp:hyp2-results}. None of the six dataset--model combinations establishes equivalence for both question-averaged causal effects and faithfulness within the prespecified margins. For MedQA with GPT-OSS 120B, faithfulness falls within the equivalence margin ($p=0.0179$), but CE does not ($p=0.9675$). None of the other five comparisons establishes faithfulness equivalence. For New German Credit, the CE and faithfulness TOST $p$-values are $1.0000$ and $0.8627$ for GPT-OSS 120B, and $0.9840$ and $0.9868$ for Mistral Medium 3.5, respectively. Consequently, the available data do not provide sufficient evidence of empirical equivalence between the two implementations.

Failure to establish equivalence does not demonstrate a statistically or practically meaningful difference. Complementary two-sided one-sample $t$-tests therefore assess whether the same paired-difference vectors used by TOST have a mean of zero. Table~\ref{tab:hyp2-difference-tests} reports unadjusted results; positive differences indicate larger FELICS estimates.

At the nominal $\alpha=0.05$ level, the CE differences are statistically significant for BBQ with GPT-OSS 120B ($p=0.0113$), MedQA with GPT-OSS 120B ($p=0.0206$), MedQA with Mistral Medium 3.5 ($p<0.0001$), and New German Credit with both GPT-OSS 120B ($p<0.0001$) and Mistral Medium 3.5 ($p=0.0047$). Faithfulness differences are statistically significant for New German Credit with GPT-OSS 120B ($p=0.0435$) and Mistral Medium 3.5 ($p=0.0019$), with larger mean faithfulness under FELICS in both cases. Thus, both New German Credit comparisons exhibit statistically significant differences in both outcomes under these unadjusted tests. The CE differences have opposite directions across the two models, so the results do not indicate a uniform increase or decrease in CE across implementations.

In summary, the evidence does not establish empirical equivalence under the evaluated runs, so Research Question~\ref{rq:convergence} cannot be answered affirmatively from these results.
