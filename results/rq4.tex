\section{Effect of Interaction Modeling on Faithfulness}
\label{sec:results-faithfulness-increase}

Research Question~\ref{rq:faithfulness_increase} examines whether the joint interventions introduced by FELICS increase question-level faithfulness relative to interventions on individual concepts. Because the vertical axis of the identity plots represents interaction-aware faithfulness, points above the identity line indicate higher faithfulness under the joint formulation.

Figures~\ref{fig:hyp4-identity-plots-natural-language} and~\ref{fig:hyp4-identity-plots} show the paired question-level estimates. For the natural-language datasets BBQ and MedQA, the points are concentrated around the identity line, indicating broadly similar atomic and interaction-aware estimates. The New German Credit panels exhibit a less pronounced correspondence between the two formulations.

The corresponding question-level mean CE comparisons are provided in Appendix Figures~\ref{fig:appendix-ce-shift-identity-natural-language} and~\ref{fig:appendix-ce-shift-identity-credit}, where marker size also records the number of concepts contributing to each question.

The paired one-sided Wilcoxon signed-rank tests did not provide evidence of systematically higher interaction-aware faithfulness for five of the six dataset–model combinations. Only New German Credit evaluated with Mistral Medium 3.5 yielded a statistically significant result. Overall, the results therefore do not reveal a consistent improvement in faithfulness across datasets and models when joint interventions are considered.

Because failure to establish an increase does not imply a decrease, a
complementary directional analysis tests the opposite alternative,
$\mathcal{F}_{\mathrm{joint}}<\mathcal{F}_{\mathrm{atomic}}$, using the same
paired one-sided Wilcoxon signed-rank procedure. Table~\ref{tab:hyp4-lower-tests}
reports these exploratory, unadjusted tests. The paired difference is defined as
$d=\mathcal{F}_{\mathrm{joint}}-\mathcal{F}_{\mathrm{atomic}}$, so a negative
median difference indicates lower interaction-aware faithfulness.

At the nominal $\alpha=0.05$ level, the analysis provides evidence of lower
interaction-aware faithfulness for BBQ with both GPT-OSS 120B ($p=0.0402$) and
Mistral Medium 3.5 ($p=0.0288$). None of the other four dataset--model
combinations supports this directional alternative. In particular, the result
for New German Credit with GPT-OSS 120B is no longer significant ($p=0.2022$).
The complementary results therefore indicate a dataset-specific decrease for
BBQ, not a general decrease across the evaluation.
