\section{Interaction-Presumption Strength and Changes in Causal Effects}
\label{sec:results-interaction-presumption}
Research Question~\ref{rq:correlation_diff} (Chapter~\ref{sec:research_questions}) asks whether the differences in question-averaged causal effects and question-level faithfulness between the joint and atomic formulations increase with the oracle's presumption of interaction among the concepts of a question. This interaction-presumption strength is operationalized by $\Delta\kappa$ (Section~\ref{sec:analysis_procedures}). It is a property of the oracle partition rather than a measure of statistical association or response-level interaction.

The correlation tests in Box~\ref{hyp:hyp1-results} identify only one supported association: for MedQA evaluated with GPT-OSS 120B, $\Delta\kappa$ is significantly positively correlated with the difference in question-averaged causal effects. None of the other dataset--model and outcome combinations meet the prespecified decision criterion. Figures~\ref{fig:rq1-medqa-gptoss-ce} and~\ref{fig:rq1-medqa-gptoss-f} show the supported combination for both outcomes. Each point represents one question, and the orange line is the least-squares linear fit. The remaining six combinations are shown in Figures~\ref{fig:app-rq1-bbq-gptoss-ce} to~\ref{fig:app-rq1-medqa-mistral-f} in Appendix~\ref{app:rq1-scatter}.

The supported association depends strongly on single questions. Question 434 has by far the largest change in CE ($\Delta\mathrm{CE}=0.023$ at $\Delta\kappa=4.67$). Without it, the Pearson correlation coefficient falls to $r=0.167$ (one-sided $p=0.198$). Question 1120 has the largest interaction-presumption strength ($\Delta\kappa=6.0$) but almost no change in CE ($\Delta\mathrm{CE}=0.002$), which lowers the linear correlation. An exploratory rank correlation, which is less sensitive to such individual values, indicates a stronger monotone association (Spearman $\rho=0.581$, one-sided $p=0.0005$). For the change in faithfulness of the same combination, the rank correlation is weaker and borderline ($\rho=0.309$, $p=0.051$). For MedQA with Mistral Medium 3.5, question 161 shows both the largest change in CE ($0.010$) and the largest change in faithfulness ($0.172$), at an intermediate $\Delta\kappa=3.0$ (Figures~\ref{fig:app-rq1-medqa-mistral-ce} and~\ref{fig:app-rq1-medqa-mistral-f}).

In BBQ, the oracle forms mostly pairs and singletons (Table~\ref{tab:oracle-summary}), so $\Delta\kappa$ varies little between questions (Figures~\ref{fig:app-rq1-bbq-gptoss-ce} to~\ref{fig:app-rq1-bbq-mistral-f}). It takes only eight distinct values between $0.33$ and $1.80$ for GPT-OSS 120B and six distinct values between $0$ and $2.20$ for Mistral Medium 3.5. For Mistral Medium 3.5, the sign of the faithfulness correlation is determined by question 8, the only question with $\Delta\kappa=2.2$: omitting it changes $r$ from $0.119$ to $-0.262$. At the same time, the largest changes in CE are considerably larger in BBQ than in MedQA ($0.21$ and $0.24$ compared with $0.023$ and $0.010$), in line with the smaller CE scale of MedQA (Table~\ref{tab:results-dataset-overview}). The results therefore do not provide consistent evidence that stronger oracle presumptions of interaction are associated with greater changes in causal effects or faithfulness when joint interventions replace atomic interventions.
